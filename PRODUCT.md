# Coy product review

## Evidence and scope

The supplied Coy screenshots show phone-number signup, first-name onboarding and the add-crush form. The signup screen explains that a phone number also lets crushes find the account. Onboarding says a first name is visible to matches and on shared content, while anonymous heads-ups omit it. The crush form accepts a private label and a phone number or email. It explains that adding a crush takes one slot, immediately sends a heads-up, and is final. The underlying screen shows a three-slot allowance and a referral progress indicator.

These observations come from the screenshots. Successful OTP delivery, saving a crush, verifying an email, opening an invite link and viewing the extra-slots screen were not independently verified. The review makes no assumptions about invite-link contents, pricing or email delivery. No purchase was made.

## 1. Review and cancel a pending crush

After someone enters a number or email, show a confirmation with the destination, the slot cost and a preview of exactly what the recipient will see. After confirmation, briefly hold delivery and show **Undo**. If the sender cancels during that window, restore the slot and discard the pending notification. Keep the sender’s private nickname out of every recipient-facing preview.

**Why Coy needs it.** The observed form combines a permanent slot decision with immediate outreach. A typo can notify a stranger before the sender realizes what happened. The review catches a wrong identifier and the brief hold makes an accidental submission recoverable before any information leaves Coy.

## 2. Control which verified identifiers can find you

Give people separate switches for matching by verified phone and by verified email. Explain the effect of each switch before saving. An inactive identifier should produce the same sender-visible response as any other submitted identifier. Re-verification should be required before activating a replacement number or email.

**Why Coy needs it.** Coy frames the phone number as both a login credential and a way for crushes to find someone. The add form also accepts email. Someone might want personal contacts to find them by phone while keeping their work email outside their dating life. Separate settings let them make that distinction without telling a sender whether a particular identifier belongs to a registered account.
